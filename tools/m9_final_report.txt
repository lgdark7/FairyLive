## 2. ABR Downward Ladder Empirical Ledger & State-Machine Rules

### A. State-Machine Downscaling Architecture & Rules
In `AdaptiveBitrateController.kt`, downward rate transitions are governed by discrete network health states designed to balance responsiveness and keyframe protection:
1. **`NetworkHealth.CRITICAL`**: Emergency clamp to the lowest ladder tier (`300 kbps`, `reason=CRITICAL_CONGESTION`, `requestKeyFrame=true`).
2. **`NetworkHealth.POOR`**: Intentional **2-tier rapid downscale** (`targetTier = (currentTierIndex + 2).coerceAtMost(3)`). Under sudden acute channel degradation (e.g. throughput < 500 kbps or latency > 350 ms), the controller intentionally drops from Tier 0 (1200 kbps) directly to Tier 2 (**500 kbps**, `reason=POOR_NETWORK`, `requestKeyFrame=true`), skipping 800 kbps. This rapid downscaling rule is by design to prevent catastrophic queue saturation and buffer bloat before the channel collapses completely.
3. **`NetworkHealth.DEGRADED`**: Single-tier step-down (`targetTier = (currentTierIndex + 1).coerceAtMost(3)`). Under mild degradation (throughput 500–800 kbps or latency 150–350 ms), the controller steps down 1 tier at a time: `1200 -> 800 kbps` (`reason=DEGRADED_LATENCY`), `800 -> 500 kbps`, and `500 -> 300 kbps`.
4. **Conservative Step-Up Recovery**: Requires 3 consecutive healthy evaluation windows (3000 ms) + 3000 ms cooldown, stepping up 1 tier at a time (`300 -> 500 -> 800 -> 1200 kbps`).
